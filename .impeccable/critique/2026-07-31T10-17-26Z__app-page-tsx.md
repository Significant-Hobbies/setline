## Design Specificity Verdict

The flow is recognizably Setline rather than generic account settings. It uses
the existing scorecard menu, factual private-copy language, coral-only
destructive semantics, square controls, and deliberate focus behavior while
keeping workout execution visually dominant.

The session's deterministic detector result remained zero findings and was not
rerun. Browser evidence at 390, 768, and 1440 pixels showed no horizontal
overflow; success/failure simulations verified preservation, cleanup, scoped
busy labels, and post-success focus.

## Overall Impression

This is a restrained privacy control that is difficult to trigger accidentally
and honest about distributed failure. Its strongest quality is outcome
separation: no response preserves the browser copy, confirmed remote deletion
cannot be downgraded to failure by local cleanup, and partial cleanup has its
own recovery copy.

## What's Working

- Better Auth owns freshness, current-user scoping, session-cookie cleanup, and
  the destructive endpoint.
- The two-step disclosure states the exact data removed before the permanent
  action is available.
- Cancel, loading, failure, confirmed success, and partial browser-cleanup
  states all retain usable focus and factual copy.

## Persona Red Flags

**Casey, distracted mobile gym user:** No red flag remains. The menu stays

**Riley, failure-path tester:** No red flag remains. A simulated 503 preserved

## Minor Observations

- Google grant revocation remains intentionally external and is named in the
  confirmation and privacy notice.
- Production deployment and live-account deletion remain outside this change.

## Questions to Consider

- Should a future account surface expose Google grant status, or is the current
