## Design Specificity Verdict

The Scoreboard Split remains clearly authored for gym execution rather than a
generic fitness dashboard. Chalk, navy rules, condensed data numerals, the lime
completion slab, and the plan/actual queue form a coherent operational
instrument. The deterministic detector returned zero findings in `app/page.tsx`.
Independent browser evidence found no unnamed controls, console errors, or
horizontal overflow at 390px and 1440px.

The browser pass did identify touch-size polish below Setline's own 44px floor:
the two Remove controls were 36px tall and the four central number inputs were
34px wide at 390px. Remove was raised to 44px after review. The narrow number
inputs remain 48px tall and are flanked by 44px decrement/increment targets; the
34px width is retained to avoid mobile overflow.

No live detector overlay was presented. The available browser path could inspect
and measure the rendered state but did not provide a reliable isolated mutable
overlay path; screenshots and DOM measurements were used instead.

## Overall Impression

The flexible player feels trustworthy and product-specific. Its biggest
opportunity is reducing decision density when a set expands, without hiding the
explicit changes the user asked for.

## What's Working

- Planned and actual truth remain visibly separate through targets, session
  queue positions, deviation labels, and the durable ledger.
- One normal set still completes with one lime action; advanced work appears only
  when the user adds a segment or chooses a deviation.
- Reload, correction, actual-rest timing, and undo create a reassuring recovery
  story in an interruption-heavy gym context.

## Priority Issues

- **P2 — Choice saturation.** A two-segment set exposes Add segment, Complete,
  Skip, Add another, and Do later together. Keep lime dominant and consider a
  quiet disclosure for the two session-deviation actions after real-use feedback.
- **P2 — Expanded desktop fold.** A two-segment editor can push Complete below
  the first 900px viewport. Preserve access to completion if real sessions show
  this causes missed or duplicate actions.
- **P2 — Small central numeric targets.** At 390px the number inputs can be 34px
  wide, though they remain 48px tall and sit between 44px stepper buttons. A
  stacked mobile segment layout is the safest future fix if direct input proves
  difficult.
- **P3 — Deviation reassurance and validation tone.** The implementation now
  adds pre-action session-only subcopy and uses a neutral incomplete prompt
  instead of an immediate red error.

## Persona Red Flags

- **Sam:** Direct number-input targets are narrow on the smallest phone layout,
  although named stepper alternatives and visible focus remain available.
- **Casey:** Two expanded segments plus secondary actions can require more
  scanning than a standard set; device-first persistence prevents interruption
  loss.
- **Riley:** Small rail metadata and unit labels may be difficult under motion or
  harsh gym lighting, but the target and entered numerals retain strong contrast.

## Minor Observations

- The queue fraction becomes actual-session progress after extras, while each
  planned row still retains its authored position.
- Removing either segment is flexible but makes segment identity more fluid than
  a fixed first/second-part model.

## Questions to Consider

- Should advanced deviations collapse behind one "Change this set" disclosure
  only after real sessions show decision fatigue?
- Should the queue eventually display authored and current-session totals
  separately after extras?
