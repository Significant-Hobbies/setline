## Design Specificity Verdict

The active player and rest transition are strongly authored for Setline. Oversized attempt notation, training-journal rules, blue recorded-data receipts, the lime completion slab, and the session rail form one coherent workout execution instrument. The generic adherence and session-quality scoring were the least specific elements.

The deterministic detector returned zero findings for `app/page.tsx`. Browser geometry caught issues outside the detector’s rules: the wordmark, View programme, and End session controls were below 44 pixels; the Start transition retained scroll position; and the 768-pixel player did not yet realize the specified two-column supporting layout. No detector false positives required suppression.

## Overall Impression

Setline’s core attempt/rest loop is visually confident and unusually legible. Its biggest opportunity is trust: a precise record must make sweaty-thumb mistakes recoverable and value correction easy.

## What’s Working

- The attempt board creates exceptional at-a-glance hierarchy without hiding previous performance, form cues, rest, or actuals.
- The completed-set receipt and rest board create a product-defining transition with the next target visible.
- Recorded, calculated, sample, unavailable, and device-local measurements remain distinguishable.
- Desktop becomes a genuine scoreboard split rather than an enlarged phone.

## Priority Issues

1. **P1 — Core recording actions are irreversible.** Complete and Skip immediately advance while the set rail is read-only. Add an undo/correction path to the just-recorded receipt and final summary.
2. **P1 — Actual-value correction is not gym-friendly.** Native number inputs are awkward one-handed and invalid values can be completed. Add 44-pixel step controls, validation, and a disabled/error state.
3. **P1 — Rest completion is assistively silent.** Give the countdown timer semantics and announce completion once without reading every tick.
4. **P2 — Touch and transition details break the physical-use promise.** Restore 44-pixel hit areas, reset scroll when opening the player, and preserve session identity on small phones.
5. **P2 — Dashboard scoring adds decisions.** Replace the sample adherence score with factual readiness and reduce session quality to a few anchored options.

## Persona Red Flags

- **Sam, accessibility-dependent:** rest completion was silent and small secondary actions missed the 44-pixel target, though form names, focus, and textual state labels were strong.
- **Casey, distracted mobile user:** correcting weight/reps required keyboard precision and starting from a scrolled Today view could hide the exercise title.
- **Programme-following lifter:** the plan, previous result, cue, and next set were all visible, but a mistaken Complete or Skip undermined trust in history.

## Minor Observations

- Coral should be reserved for the final rest-warning window rather than the full countdown.
- Active set time can be shown as unavailable more quietly in the summary.
- At 768 pixels the session rail should support the active board rather than sit below it.

## Questions to Consider

- Should the completed-set receipt be the primary correction surface?
- What should “session quality” mean in a product that avoids making training decisions?
- How much of the full set rail is useful on a phone before it becomes noise?
